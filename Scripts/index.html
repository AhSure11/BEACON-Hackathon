03 / MAKE IT REALPROJECT GUIDANCE

One real task.
One working
agent.

Keep your scope focused enough to demonstrate. A small, thoughtful project can tell a big story.

New to agents? Start here ↗
01

Give it a goal.

Define the person, their problem, and what a successful result looks like.

02

Let it do useful work.

Use a tool, a dataset, or a workflow. Show how the agent chooses its next step and handles changing information.

03

Keep people in control.

Make the agent's actions understandable. Build in review when it is about to send, spend, or change something important.

04

Demonstrate the difference.

Walk through a realistic example. Show what worked, what you measured, and where the agent still needs help.

Here is what to know so far.

What is AgentHacks?

AgentHacks is a virtual hackathon being planned by TAMS BEACON. This challenge preview explores building AI agents that help people complete real tasks.

What do you mean by an AI agent?

For this proposed challenge, an agent works toward a goal by choosing and carrying out steps, using tools or information, and responding to the results. Think of a system that helps assemble a usable plan, prepare a workflow, or finish a task.

Do I choose a social or business track?

The proposed theme brings these together under one challenge: turn a real goal into a completed task. Everyday life, community, and work are sources of inspiration. A project can fit more than one.

Is this beginner-friendly?

You can begin exploring a focused idea without a complex system: choose one user, one task, and one useful tool. Eligibility, team rules, and any beginner resources will be confirmed with the event announcement.

When is it, and how do I register?

The event will be virtual. Dates, registration, eligibility, team sizes, and submission details are still to be announced. Registration is not yet available on this page.

What are the judging criteria and prizes?

The official rubric and any prizes have not been announced. The project guidance above is a suggested way to approach the challenge, not a finalized judging rubric.
